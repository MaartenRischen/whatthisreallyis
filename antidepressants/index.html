What this really is
  • Depression without a biomarker is the human brain pulling effort off a life where effort has stopped changing anything.
  • Humans are built to lie low when nothing they do works, and to get going again when it does.
  • Modern hierarchies are run by strangers nobody can simply walk away from.
  • The pull-back is built to switch off when a person's own actions work again or trusted people close in.
  • Without a biomarker, calling low mood a serious illness is a label, and the label explains nothing.

England's antidepressant boom

Depression without a biomarker is the human brain doing its job: pulling effort off a life where effort has stopped changing anything, trusted people are thin and standing is low. In modern England, work, rent and benefits are decided by people nobody knows and nobody can walk away from. The pull-back saves energy and risk for goals that pay off in survival, mates and children, and switches off when effort pays again or a person moves camp, and there neither comes.

Source: Medicines Used in Mental Health - England 2016/17 to 2025/26: Antidepressant drugs (BNF 4.3), NHS Business Services Authority (NHSBSA), 23 July 2026

The pull-back fires where effort stops showing results. For most of human history, work was done in small groups with the hands, its results were plain to see, and adults settled group decisions by discussion. Women are 65% of patients and 71% of those aged 15 to 19. A mother's commitment to each child tracks the help and food around her, and forager mothers had fathers, grandmothers and older children helping, where today one or two adults carry most of the care; no study here tests whether that drives the skew.

Forager bands had no rulers, and anyone could walk to another camp without penalty, which changed every input at once. Standing came from skill and sharing, granted by people who knew you, and better hunters and storytellers had more children.

In a band, the pull-back eased when a person's own actions worked again or a small inner circle of trusted people closed in. England's answer is a prescription, and the release counts 44% more patients in the most deprived areas than in the least.

Calling it a serious illness when no biomarker shows a physical cause stops the explanation at a label. Where a thyroid fault or inflammation with a marker exists, the low mood is a symptom of that cause. The release admits its 9.1 million patients include people prescribed for migraine, chronic pain and ME, since it records no reason for any prescription.

So now what?

  • Join or start a small group where your effort shows a result you can see that week, such as a shared allotment, a repair group or a team job with people you know.
  • Put the same few trusted people in the same room every week, a meal or a walk on a fixed day, so someone from your own life shares the load.
  • Get together with neighbours in the same street or building to take one demand to a landlord or the council, so the people who set your terms face a group that knows each other.

If you take psychiatric medication: where no biomarker shows a physical disease, the goal is to come off it. Work on the real cause, most often your situation with other people, at the same time as a slow taper supervised by your doctor. Never stop suddenly: stopping some drugs at once is dangerous.

What cuts against it

  • Nesse holds that low mood often escalates into depression that serves no purpose, and that psychotic depression comes from broken mood regulation. An immune response alone produces the same withdrawal in animals, whatever the controllability. The control circuit is charted mainly in rodents, and human brain imaging support is thin. Ancestral life also carried losses no action could prevent: about 49 percent of forager children died before 15.
