Falling work engagement

Falling engagement is the human organism refusing to work hard for a group that can't see it. Remote and hybrid work leaves your effort unseen by anyone who knows you, under rules you had no say in. For most of human history everyone in camp saw who brought food home, and that record decided who shared with you and who wanted you as a partner.

Source: U.S. Employee Engagement Below 30% for First Time in 15 Years, Gallup.com, 28 Oct 2026

NOW: a remote or hybrid worker alone at a home desk with a laptop, a manager visible only on a screen, and an AI tool on the screen.

The drop tracks distance from other people. Engagement fell five points among fully remote employees, two among hybrid staff and about one on-site. For most of human history what you brought in came back to a camp of a few dozen people who knew you. Remote work cuts out that camp, and the drop is largest where it is cut most.

Remote work also removes the people you lean on. For most of human history close support came from a small inner circle nested inside a camp of a few dozen familiar people. Only 37 percent strongly agree someone at work cares about them, and even the 47 percent of March 2020 sits far below a camp where close support was a few steps away.

Rules from above add the last piece. Forager bands had no rulers, and a would-be boss got mocked or left behind for another camp. Today the employer decides how AI reshapes each role, only 46 percent strongly agree they know what is expected, and quitting costs the income, so the brain falls back to its default of lying low.

Gallup's own series undercuts its headline. Engagement sat between 26 and 30 percent from 2000 through 2013, so 29 percent is no new floor, and none of those years comes near a camp where everyone saw what you brought home. In forager bands food went to others because they demanded it, and stinginess drew gossip and ridicule from people who knew you. Gallup, which sells engagement consulting, answers with clearer AI plans from the same distant authority.

So now what?

  • Work alongside the same few colleagues in one room on fixed days each week, so the same faces see your work day after day.
  • Hand finished work over in person to the people it serves, with a demo or a walk-through, so the result lands in front of people who know you.
  • Keep a small standing circle of two to five colleagues you talk things over with face to face or by phone, and keep meeting them when roles change.

If you take psychiatric medication: where no biomarker shows a physical disease, the goal is to come off it. Work on the real cause, most often your situation with other people, at the same time as a slow taper supervised by your doctor. Never stop suddenly: stopping some drugs at once is dangerous.
