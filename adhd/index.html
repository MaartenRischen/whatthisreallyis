The ADHD count

ADHD is the restless, novelty-chasing drive that sent children out after food for most of human history, and school hands it a chair and nothing to find. A child built to move through a mixed-age group, playing, watching and gathering its own food, now sits at set work under one adult in a class born the same year. Rough play, the rehearsal for courtship and rivals, gets cut too, and the children whose drive runs strongest, boys most, are the ones counted in the 13.8 percent.

Source: Percentage of Children Who Had Ever Received an ADHD Diagnosis, Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, 29 Oct 2026

The CDC figure counts labels. In household interviews, the share of American children aged 5 to 17 ever diagnosed rose from 9.9 percent in 2019 to 13.8 percent in 2025. Genes do not shift that fast, so the labelling or the setting changed, and the report examines neither.

For most of human history, children past infancy spent the day in a mixed-age group that largely ran itself, learning by playing, watching and joining in, and gathering a considerable share of their own food. School swaps that for a class born the same year, one adult who decides the task, the pace and when anyone may stand, and worksheets graded later. In a room built for stillness, the drive to explore, move and play comes out as fidgeting, drifting and acting on impulse.

How hard that drive pushes runs in families and differs from child to child. Forager children played in one mixed-age group, and sorting children by birth year under one adult turns that spread into a ranking, with the most restless flagged first. A forager could walk to another camp, and a child cannot leave the room.

Where a measurable physical cause exists, such as a brain injury or poisoning, the restlessness is a symptom of it, and in every other child the label names a heritable drive stuck in a chair.

So now what?

  • Give the child daily time outdoors in a group of children of different ages who run their own physical play, with no adult directing it: neighbours, older cousins, a street or park group.
  • Ask the school for lessons that let children stand, walk and work with their hands, and for permission to get up without asking.
  • Hand the child real jobs with a result they can hold the same day: cooking a meal, building something, growing food, running an errand.

If you take psychiatric medication: where no biomarker shows a physical disease, the goal is to come off it. Work on the real cause, most often your situation with other people, at the same time as a slow taper supervised by your doctor. Never stop suddenly: stopping some drugs at once is dangerous.

  • Constructed-emotion research finds no consistent brain or body fingerprint for any single emotion and reads so-called emotion regions as body-budgeting regions, which disputes treating seeking as one dedicated circuit.
