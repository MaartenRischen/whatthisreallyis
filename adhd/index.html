What it really is

Restless people, made to sit still.

ADHD is a name for people who move a lot and notice everything, once school or an office puts them in a chair all day.

The drive to move on and look for the next thing is 460 million years old, and people have always differed in how much they need to move.
Humans have existed for 300,000 years, and for nearly all of that time children played in groups with no adult in charge. Then people started farming and settling down, and much later they put children in classrooms. Farming began 12,000 years ago. Now doctors record the failure to sit still on a behaviour checklist and call it ADHD.

How it happens

  1. 1

    Some people need to move more than others.

    The science says

    People differ in how much they need to move and how fast their attention jumps.

    In a foraging game, people with high attention-deficit scores moved on sooner and collected more.

So now what

  1. Walk for 20 minutes before each long sitting task.
  2. Cut work into short pieces that each show a result.
  3. For a child, add free play with children of other ages.

If you or your child takes medication for this: where no test shows a physical disease, the aim is to replace the drug with the real thing: a day with movement in it and work that comes in short pieces. The drug goes as that comes in. Bring the dose down with a doctor while you build it. It doesn't have to be the doctor who prescribed it. Some drugs are dangerous to stop at once, so never stop suddenly.

Send this to the parent who has been told their seven-year-old has a brain disorder because he can't sit still.

The 9 details
  1. Doctors diagnose it with a behaviour checklist.
  2. No blood test or scan shows it.
  3. The youngest in a class get it a third more often.
  4. It shows most at school and in the office.
  5. The same person focuses for hours on a job that moves.
  6. Fidgeting and leaving the seat are core items.
  7. It runs in families.
  8. Stimulants help people sit still.

What cuts against it

  • Foragers rest about as many hours a day as people in industrial societies. The difference Cor records is posture and how movement is spread through the day, so "they never sat" would be wrong.
