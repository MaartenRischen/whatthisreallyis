    The evolutionary angle: what their brains see

    Missile strikes read as raiders on the hill above the camp. When another group attacked, a band survived by standing close together round the fire.

    The mismatch

    Here the old response still fits. A threat from outside pulls people closer, and going out together under attack is that response. What doesn't fit is the length: a raid ended, and months of strikes don't.

    The mechanisms at work

    Cor ranks the inputs to each mechanism on a proxy gradient, from the one that fully meets the need (1) down to the one that meets the least (5). On the right: Cor's evidence tag for a step, where it gives one.

    In Cor, bonding turns the threat system down: being with your people quiets the alarm.

    So now what?

    1. Keep doing it together. Staying close to your people is the part of the old response that still works.
    2. Give the alarm an end each day. Check the news at set times, then put it away.
    3. Do things with a result you can see. Fixing, cooking or helping a neighbour gives the body something to act on.
