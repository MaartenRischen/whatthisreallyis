The cost-benefit analysis

Your brain does a cost-benefit analysis on every bond. A friend costs time, favours you return, and the risk of being turned down. What a friend brings in is someone who comes when you need help. For nearly all of human history every face you saw and heard often belonged to someone like that, so a familiar face was worth the bond. A face on a screen costs almost nothing. It is there every day, it talks to you, and it can't turn you down. It lifts your mood about as much as a friend does. It brings in nobody who knows you and nobody who comes. The cheap face wins on cost, so the brain bonds with it. What changed is the price of the real route. The few dozen people who knew you all your life are gone, friends take effort to see, and a company puts the face in your ear every morning.

So now what

  1. Count who knows you back. Write down the people you saw or spoke to this week who would notice if you were gone.
  2. Go to the show with people, or talk to the people next to you. The star is the stand-in. The friends you make there are real.
  3. When a famous face dies, mourn with other people in person. The people you mourn with are real.
  4. Move one daily hour with a podcast or a stream to a call with a friend. A friend talks back, and comes when you need help.
  5. Don't pay a star for a video. It buys a minute in which a stranger says your name.
  6. If you are the famous one, keep the friends from before, and keep them off the payroll.
  7. If a famous face is the person you hear most, take that as the signal. What is missing is people who know you back.
