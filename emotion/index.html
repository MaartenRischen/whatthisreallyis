What it really is

Your body ordering you to do what kept your ancestors alive and got them children.

An emotion is a state your whole body switches into when something happens that used to decide who lived and who had children: a threat, a loss, a cheat, a willing mate, a crying baby. It changes what you notice, what you remember, what you want and what your heart does, all at once, and it pushes you towards one action.Fear gets you away from danger. Anger makes a cheat pay. Shame tells you people are starting to think less of you. Emotions are not there to make you happy. For nearly all of human history each one went off at something close by, in front of a few dozen people who knew you, and they did something about it.Now it goes off at a debt, an inbox and strangers on a screen. You can't fight any of them or run from them, and nobody around you notices, so it stays on. Then you are told your emotions are the problem and sold a way to manage them.

The part of you that makes you freeze, run or fight back is 460 million years old, and every animal with a backbone has it.
The part that makes you miss your people is 165 million years old, and every mammal has it. Humans have existed for 300,000 years, and for nearly all of that time you lived in a camp of a few dozen people who knew you. What set off an emotion was near you, and the people around you could see it on your face. Then people started farming and settling down, and villages grew into towns and cities. That began 12,000 years ago. Now you spend the day among strangers. What sets you off is a deadline, a debt or a post by someone you will never meet. The emotion has nothing to act on and nobody answers it, and people call that poor emotional regulation.

In three countries, how much shame people expected to feel in 29 situations matched how much other people there would think less of someone in each one. The feeling had the answer already. An emotion also picks what you look at, what you remember and what you want, so your thinking happens inside it.

How it happens

  1. 1

    A few situations decided who lived and who had children.

    The science says

    A few situations decided whether your ancestors lived and whether they had children: danger, a loss, a cheat, a chance at a mate, a child who needs you.

    A chance at something brings excitement, getting it brings joy, a threat brings anxiety and a loss brings sadness. The good feelings from winning are about balanced by the bad feelings from losing. The system is set to spread your genes. It was never set to make you happy.

    1 sourceargued Randolph M. Nesse (2019). Good Reasons for Bad Feelings: Insights from the Frontier of Evolutionary Psychiatry.
  2. so2

    For each one your body has a ready state. That state is an emotion.

    The science says

    For each of those situations your body has a ready state. It takes over your attention, memory, wants, heart and muscles together and pushes you towards one action. That state is an emotion.

    Take fear. When you think someone is following you, you hear small sounds you would normally miss. Safety pushes every other goal aside. Your mouth goes dry because digestion stops, blood moves to your legs and you breathe faster. You decide none of it. Jaak Panksepp counted seven such systems in mammals: seeking, fear, rage, lust, care, panic at separation and play. The same brain paths and chemicals run them in every mammal, and animals keep all seven after the outer layer of the brain is removed.

    Each one does a job, and several are messages to other people.

    The science says

    Each state does a job, and several are messages to other people. Anger makes a cheat pay. Shame shows the others you know you lost standing. Crying brings help.

    In experiments people paid their own money to punish someone who cheated the group, with nothing to gain. Anger at the cheat drove it. Where punishing was allowed, cooperation held. Where it was ruled out, cooperation fell apart. Shame tracks what other people think. In three countries the shame people expected to feel matched how much others would think less of them. Looking down and blushing tell the others you accept the lower place, which makes them less likely to keep attacking. Guilt makes you repair what you broke, and gratitude makes you pay back. A young mammal separated from its mother cries and searches for her.

    So it stays on.

    The science says

    Now an emotion goes off at things you can't fight, run from or fix: a debt, an inbox, strangers on a screen. Nobody around you notices, and you are told your emotions are the problem.

    Debt, emails and social media can't be fought or run from, so nothing ends the alarm. You now see more unfamiliar faces in a day than a forager may have met in a lifetime. In 2.7 million Facebook and Twitter posts, each word about the other political side went with 67 percent higher odds of a share, and with angry reactions. An alarm that never switches off damages the heart and blood vessels, the immune system and a memory area of the brain.

So now what

  1. When a strong feeling comes, ask what set it off before you try to make it stop. What happened, who did it, and what did you lose or nearly lose?
  2. Where you can change what set it off, change that: the person, the job, the debt.
  3. Feel it next to someone who knows you, in the same room. A phone call does more than a text.
  4. Cut what sets you off and leaves you nothing to do about it. Posts about what the other side did get shared more and draw angry reactions, and there is nothing you can do with that anger.
  5. See a doctor if a strong feeling started suddenly with no change in your life, or soon after a new pill. Infections, hormone problems, drugs and head injuries can set off fear or low mood.

If you take pills for anxiety or low mood, talk to the prescriber before you change anything, and never stop suddenly. If you are thinking about ending your life, tell someone today and call your local emergency number.

  • Researchers have looked for the mark of each emotion in the face, the body and the brain, and found no single pattern for any of them. What would prove this wrong

If how strongly people feel an emotion had nothing to do with what is at stake for them, this page is wrong.

It has been tested for shame. In three countries and in 15 small communities, the shame people expected to feel matched how much others would think less of them.

Strongest objection"I panic in supermarkets and nothing there can hurt me. How is that a signal?" A false alarm is the system working as built. It goes off far more often than it needs to, in everyone, because missing one real danger cost as much as a thousand needless scares. Some people's goes off more easily, and that difference is inherited. A supermarket is also a crowd of strangers, and for 300,000 years a stranger was rare and could be dangerous. Check for a physical cause first.
