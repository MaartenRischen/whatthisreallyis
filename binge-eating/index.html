What it really is

Eating what is in reach until it is gone. Fat kept people alive and able to have children, and the food used to run out first. Now it never runs out.

Binge eating is the name for eating a lot in a short time, past full, usually alone. The body eats what is in reach and stores the rest as fat.     The body eats what is in reach and stores fat.

    The science says

    The body eats what is in reach and stores the rest as fat. Humans carry more of it than any other ape, and it kept them alive and able to have children.

    Measured with doubly labelled water, humans burned about 400 to 820 calories a day more than the other great apes, size and activity allowed for, and had the highest share of body fat. Dissected bonobos had almost no fat on the males and at most 8.6 percent on the females, where Hadza women carry 19 percent, and the authors conclude that storing fat came to matter more and more for having children as humans evolved. The genes that move body weight are mostly active in the brain, the hypothalamus among them.

The cost-benefit analysis

Your brain does a cost-benefit analysis every time food is in reach. Eating it costs a little effort and, past full, some discomfort. Leaving it cost a lot for nearly all of human history: a kill was finished by the people who came to it, honey was eaten where it was found, and nothing could be kept, so food you left was food you lost. What you ate went into fat, and fat kept people alive and able to have children. So the brain picks eating it now. Back then the analysis ended by itself, because the food ran out. Now the food never runs out, it is built to go down fast, and it waits in the freezer, so leaving it costs nothing. The brain still counts the old way and keeps picking the same route.

In reality, I was lonely.

Victoria Ferguson, “Confronting my binge eating disorder”, Dazed, 26 February 2019

As a child she ate when hungry and stopped when full. At sleepovers she and her best friend pooled their pocket money on chocolate and ice cream and ate until they couldn't eat any more, and she remembers it as a happy time. On the weekends without a sleepover her siblings were at their father's and her mother was out. Alone in front of the television she ate multipacks of biscuits and boxes of cereal. Next So now what

So now what

  1. Eat with people. Make one meal a day a meal with someone who knows you: a housemate, a friend, family. Cook it together and split it.
  2. Keep the house short. Buy food for the next meal or two, and leave the binge foods in the shop. A walk to the shop puts work back between hunger and food.
  3. Take the delivery apps off your phone.
  4. Tell one person what happens when you are alone. Shame keeps it hidden, and hiding it keeps you alone.
  5. Fill the kitchen with food you have to cook: meat, fish, eggs, beans, vegetables, fruit. It goes down slower, and the brake has time to catch it.
  6. Don't starve to make up for it. A diet tells the body food is short, and the body pushes back for more than a year.

If you take a drug for this, such as the stimulant Vyvanse: where no physical defect is the cause, the aim is to replace the drug with the real thing, which is meals with people who know you, from food that runs out. The drug comes down as that comes in, because the real thing is hard to build while you are on it. Never stop suddenly. Stopping some of these drugs, and alcohol, at once is dangerous, so do that with a doctor. If you have been hungry all the time and putting on weight since early childhood, ask a doctor about a gene test: a broken leptin or MC4R gene is a disease, and leptin treats one of them.

What backs it

  • Eating a lot when food came in was normal. Among the Hadza, large carcasses were butchered on the spot and much of the meat was eaten at the kill site, sometimes over several hours, by the men, women and children who gathered there. Most honey was eaten where it was collected.

    1 source: Hawkes et al. 2001. Full listClosetested Hawkes K, O'Connell JF, Blurton Jones NG (2001). Hadza meat sharing. Evolution and Human Behavior 22.

  • Dieting is no cause of it. Among Fijian schoolgirls, the share who had dieted went from rare to 69 percent in three years of television, and the share who reported binges stayed level, at 7.9 and then 4.6 percent.

    1 source: Becker et al. 2002. Full listClosetested Becker AE, Burwell RA, Gilman SE, Herzog DB, Hamburg P (2002). Eating behaviours and attitudes following prolonged exposure to television among ethnic Fijian adolescent girls. British Journal of Psychiatry 180.

  • A real defect looks different. Children born with no working leptin ate constantly. On leptin injections they ate 45 to 84 percent less, and each time their bodies made antibodies against the injected leptin, the overeating came back until the dose went up.

    1 source: Farooqi et al. 2002. Full listClosetested Farooqi IS, Matarese G, Lord GM and others (2002). Beneficial effects of leptin on obesity, T cell hyporesponsiveness, and neuroendocrine/metabolic dysfunction of human congenital leptin deficiency. Journal of Clinical Investigation 110.

What cuts against it

  • Forager life was no cycle of feast and famine. In a sample of societies across the world, foragers had famine less often than farmers and herders, and the ethnography shows them no shorter of food than farmers. So the page cannot say ancestors binged to get through famines. What the records carry is that food came in lumps, a kill or a find of honey, and was eaten when it came.

What would prove this wrong

If people who eat with others every day, from food that has to be bought, carried and cooked, binge as often as people who eat alone with food always in reach, this page is wrong.

A study can settle this.

Strongest objection"The food is everywhere, for everyone. Why don't we all binge?" Most people do eat more than foragers did, and 57 percent of what American adults eat is ultra-processed. Bingeing comes where more is added: the person eats alone, has dieted, or was born with a stronger pull. Genes set how easily it starts and how big it gets. The supply decides whether anything stops it.
