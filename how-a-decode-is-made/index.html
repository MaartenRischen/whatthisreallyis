The three checks, for every finding in Cor, the evidence record behind this site:

  1. Every finding is checked against the study's full text or abstract. Summaries, search snippets and Wikipedia don't count.
  2. The finding says no more than the study does.
  3. Every grade names the strongest study behind it and that study's main weakness.

A grade never goes up because it would help a page. A finding that failed to replicate is marked down, however widely it is cited. Cor keeps records of the evidence against itself, publishes the strongest attacks on its own work and names the results that would kill it. It's, in a word, really good science.

What every decode starts from

Seven starting points

They are stated here so you can reject them knowingly.

  1. 1

    Survival and reproduction come first.Every action, feeling, want and habit comes from a system that helped your ancestors stay alive, have children, or help their relatives do it. "I just like it" never ends an explanation.

  2. 2

    The measure is how humans lived for nearly all of their history.That life shows what humans need. Nobody has to go back to it. What counts is what that life supplied, never the form it took.

  3. 3

    The human works.A reaction is a signal about the person's situation. The cause sits in a world that stopped supplying what the systems were built for.

  4. 4

    What people say about themselves is data.A stated reason, a label, a diagnosis or a self-description gets decoded and is never adopted.

  5. 5

    A modern number is compared with how humans lived, never with another modern group.In a study of modern people, "normal" already means the mismatched life.

  6. 6

    Only a clear physical defect is a disease, found or not yet found.A marker that only shows how sensitive a person is to stimuli and mismatch is no defect. Where no defect is the cause, the reaction is a normal human response to a mismatched world. Genes set how easily it comes and how strong it is. Where Cor has a record of what being born with a high setting got the person, the decode says what it got them. No label is left out of this, psychosis and bipolar included.

  7. 7

    Cor decides.A decode is right when it follows from Cor's records. Nobody's opinion settles it, the founder's included.

One by one

The 12 steps

Each step shows what it did on one real page, Friendship, which was made with version 4 of this method on 23 November 2026. Where a step leaves something you can see on a decode page, that is named too. In the steps, "the writer" is the AI that writes the decode.

Read itSteps 1 to 3

1

Save the source

The writer saves the thing being decoded, as a working link or word for word, and gives every detail in it a number. For a word like friendship that means four or five different cases. The numbered list is what the decode has to explain.

On the pageThe list of details.

FriendshipAn AI that had never seen the page gathered five first-person accounts: making a friend as an adult, a friend who never reaches out, money lent to a friend of 22 years, a best friendship that faded after a move, and a friend who showed up in a crisis. Together they hold 235 numbered details. The page lists nine that the accounts show, starting with "Losing a friend hurts."

2

Ask what the thing is

The thing is either someone's own account of their life or something a business made to sell. If it was made to sell, the decode asks who sells, who pays, and what was blown up past real life. Every decode also asks who earns money from the reaction and who earns money from the wrong answers.

On the page"Who sells the wrong answers".

FriendshipThe page names friendship apps, a site that rents out friends by the hour, and AI companion apps. It adds: "No one earns money when two neighbours help each other move."

3

Translate the word

The writer rewrites the modern word as what the human animal is doing or missing, and ties it to a system in Cor and to Cor's record of how humans lived. It searches Cor by what a thing does, never by its label.

6

Rank them, and say why in words

A reading is ranked on three grounds: what is at stake according to Cor, survival before reproduction, how many of the details it explains, and how far today's situation is from how humans lived. The page shows which details each system explains and gives the reason for its lead in words. It prints no score, because the ranking is a judgment. A system's age orders the list and settles a close call. What led on an earlier page counts for nothing. On a page about a trait or a diagnosis, where what having more of the trait got the person ranks first, it is the main claim of the page. It leads on no page by default.

8

Write the claims at full force

The top line says what the thing is. The writer states each cause as something the world does. The top line names both payoffs, survival and reproduction, where both apply. On a page about a trait or a diagnosis where Cor has a record of what having more of the trait got the person, the visible top text says it, and the page's record of its check names that Cor record. Every scene from the life humans lived comes from a record in Cor, never from memory or a hunch.

On the pageThe top line and every claim under it.

FriendshipThe top line is "Two people who help each other survive, get laid, or both."

9

Check every line against Cor

The writer sets each sentence beside the record it rests on and asks two things: does the line say more than the record, and could it say more. A line that Cor contradicts is replaced by the true line, said just as hard.

Reads Cor

On the pageA source line under each claim.

FriendshipThe page said: "Friends share no genes, so the help has to come back." Cor's records say friends are mostly not close relatives, and some are relatives. The page now says: "Friends are mostly not relatives, so the help has to come back."

10

Show what cuts against it

The page lists the strongest evidence against itself and names the result that would prove it wrong. A study counts against a page only if it would be good enough to count for it. On a page about a trait or a diagnosis, the strongest objection to what the trait got the person stands on the page with its answer. The first one to answer is Keller and Miller 2006, "Resolving the paradox of common, harmful, heritable mental disorders: Which evolutionary genetic models work best?", Behavioral and Brain Sciences. It argues that many rare mutations, each mildly harmful, explain common heritable diagnoses, with no advantage needed. It is an argument from a model, with no data of its own, so each page answers it and does not count it against itself.

Reads Cor

The rules for the writing

  • The claim is said flat. The check against Cor never adds a "maybe". It sorts a claim four ways: Cor says it in other words, Cor has a gap, the checker misread, or Cor says the opposite. Only the last changes the claim.
  • A missing study is a gap in Cor. It goes on Cor's list of what it needs. It is never printed beside a claim as a weakness.
  • A study's size, year and weak points stay with its reference. The evidence against a page sits in one place on that page, complete.
  • An animal study never stands alone as proof about people. The line says why it carries over: which system it is, that humans share it and since when by Cor's record, and what Cor holds on the same system in people. A shared system is a reason to expect the same in people, and the line states it as that. Where Cor has neither a record of the shared system nor a study in people, the animal finding comes off the page.
  • A page about a trait or a diagnosis says what having more of the trait got the person. In its visible top text it says what having more of the trait than most people got the person who had it, in the life humans lived for nearly all of their history, including what it earned them from the people around them. Then it says what the same trait runs into today. That line comes from a record in Cor and is stated bluntly as this project's position. Where Cor has no record, the page has no such line until it does. The full-blown state, such as a full high or psychosis, is never called useful. It is the trait with nothing to answer it. In "How it happens", the step that says some people are born with more of the trait also says what that got them.
  • A reading is stated bluntly as this project's position. It is never presented as a measured result.
  • Between two correct lines the more provocative one wins.
  • What "everyone knows" is a claim to check. So is what a guideline says, and so is what the AI remembers. None of them stands until the primary studies are read. The same check is run on our own claims.
  • On medication a page says the same thing every time, whole. The drug gets replaced by the real thing, such as people who need you and give back. The dose comes down while the real thing comes in. Where stopping suddenly is dangerous, as with certain drugs and alcohol, never stop suddenly, and do it with a doctor.

Every change, dated

Changes to the method

  1. Version 8

    24 November 2026Two steps were dropped, and the method now has 12. Both were done by a second AI. One ranked the readings without seeing the writer's reasoning. The other checked the finished page sentence by sentence against its sources. On 24 November 2026 that second check failed lines on eight pages, among them lines that were true, and the lines were weakened. All of them were put back the same day. The second AI did not have the reasoning behind a line. A line on a page changes when a record in Cor says the opposite, as the first writing rule says.

  2. Version 7

    24 November 2026What having more of a trait got the person became one of the readings that are listed and ranked. Where it ranks first, it is the main claim of the page. The step in "How it happens" that says some people are born with more of the trait now says what that got them, and starting point 6 says the same. The strongest objection to that reading stands on the page with its answer.

  3. Version 6

    24 November 2026A new rule says that a page about a trait or a diagnosis says what having more of the trait got the person in the life humans lived, and what it earned them from the people around them. The line has to come from a record in Cor. The full-blown state is never called useful.

  4. Version 5

    24 November 2026Starting point 6 now also says: found or not yet found, a marker of sensitivity is no defect, psychosis and bipolar by name. The rule on medication now names the real thing, people who need you and give back, and puts the doctor where a sudden stop is dangerous. The outside reader of this page found both lines shorter in the method than in the project's settled positions.

  5. Version 4

    23 November 2026A new rule says that an animal study never stands alone as proof about people.

  6. Version 3

    23 November 2026"Standing" was dropped as a level of what is at stake. Cor has no such level and files the status system under survival.

  7. Version 2

    23 November 2026The pages stopped printing a score. Its inputs were set by the writer, so the number looked more exact than it was. The ranking is now given in words.

  8. Version 1

    23 November 2026The method was written down in one place for the first time. New with it: the version number, the rule that every session that makes a decode names it, and the rule that every page prints it.

  9. Before 1

    5 to 22 November 2026The rules came in over these days. 5 November: a fixed procedure, with scores. 6 November: age taken out of the score, "what is the thing" and the money check added. 7 November: nothing carries over from earlier decodes, for or against a reading. 8 November: self-labels are decoded. 10 November: words as well as cases, Cor read first, Cor as the standard. 16 November: translate first, full force, the line check. 18 November: the settled positions. 21 November: one standard for evidence on both sides, and mismatch as the measure. 22 November: the writer's check is no check, and what "helps" means.
