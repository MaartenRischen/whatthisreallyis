What it really is

A name for not fitting in.

Neurodivergent is what you get called, or call yourself, when school, the office and a life among strangers don't fit you. That life fits no human. No test can tell a neurodivergent brain from any other. The name gets you a group to belong to and a reason that isn't your fault.It also sets you apart. Standing out is how humans get picked as mates and allies, and it used to take skill and effort. The name does it for free.

The part of you that needs allies is 25 million years old, and the part that tracks where you stand is 420 million.
Humans have existed for 300,000 years, and for nearly all of that time you knew almost everyone you met. They knew what you were like, and you stood out by what you did in front of them. Then people started farming and settling down, and villages grew into towns and cities. That began 12,000 years ago. Much later came schools and offices that ask the same thing of everyone. Now you sit among strangers who have never seen you do anything, and the demands don't fit. A diagnosis gives that a name, and the name makes you someone.

How it happens

  1. 1

    People differ, and the differences are inherited.

    The science says

    People differ in how much they move, how their attention works and how easily they read other people. The differences are inherited.

    Autism is about 80 percent heritable and ADHD about 75 percent. Every behavioural trait that has been measured is partly inherited.

    The ones who fit worst look like failures.

    The science says

    The people who fit worst fall behind. Among strangers nobody knows why, so it looks like their own fault.

    Shame follows what the people around you think of you. In three countries, the shame people expected to feel matched how much others would look down on them.

  5. so5

    A diagnosis takes the blame away and comes with a group.

    The science says

    A diagnosis gives it a name. The name takes the blame away and comes with a group to belong to.

    Austrian psychologists report that more young adults now arrive at an assessment having already diagnosed themselves, most often with ADHD or autism. The reasons the psychologists see are relief from guilt, an explanation, and a group to belong to. A camp could tell who couldn't from who wouldn't, because everyone saw everyone. Boehm records that foragers who dodged their share by pretending to be hurt got gossip and ridicule. Strangers can't tell, so a diagnosis has to.

    It also sets you apart, for free.

    The name also sets you apart. Standing out gets a human mates and allies, and it used to cost skill and effort. A label costs nothing.

So now what

  1. Write down what in your day doesn't fit you: the sitting, the noise, the strangers, the tasks someone else chose. Change the ones you can.
  2. See the same few people every week, in person, until they know what you're like.
  3. Get good at something those people need, and do it where they can see it.

Talk to the prescriber before changing any medication. Ask a doctor about a physical cause too: in some people diagnosed with autism, a gene test finds one.

What cuts against it

  • In some people diagnosed with autism, a gene test finds a known genetic syndrome. That is a physical cause, and the word neurodivergent puts those people in one group with everyone else it covers.

    1 sourcetested Abi-Dargham A, Moeller SJ, Ali F and others (2023). Candidate biomarkers in psychiatric disorders: state of the field. World Psychiatry 22.

  • Two of 107 Hadza children met the criteria for ADHD in parent interviews, and both also met the criteria for autism. Some children stand out in a forager camp too.

    1 sourcetested Ougrin D, Woodhouse E, Tucker G, Ronaldson A, Bakolis I (2023). The Prevalence of Behavioural Symptoms and Psychiatric Disorders in Hadza Children. Scientific Reports 13.

  • Keller and Miller argue that common psychiatric diagnoses come from many small harmful mutations. On that reading the far ends of these traits are damage, and ordinary variation is the wrong word for them.

What would prove this wrong

If doctors find a scan or blood test that separates neurodivergent brains from the rest, this page is wrong.

A review of 780 studies looked for one in children and found none.

Strongest objection"So you're saying autistic people are faking?" No. The differences are real and inherited, and school and office life is hard on the people who have them. The invented part is two kinds of brain.
