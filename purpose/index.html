Cor is the core of this project: an evidence atlas of what humans need, drawn from thousands of studies in evolutionary biology, anthropology, primatology, neuroscience, psychology and psychiatry. Every decode, every page and every Decode Talk conversation is made from it. AI compiles the studies into Cor and uses Cor to do the decoding, more objectively than any human can.

The three checks, for every finding in Cor:

  1. Every finding is checked against the study's full text or abstract. Summaries, search snippets and Wikipedia don't count.
  2. The finding says no more than the study does.
  3. Every grade names the strongest study behind it and that study's main weakness.

A grade never goes up because it would help a page. A finding that failed to replicate is marked down, however widely it is cited. Cor keeps records of the evidence against itself, publishes the strongest attacks on its own work and names the results that would kill it.

What it really is

Being needed.

Purpose is the feeling of being needed by people you know. For most of human history, humans survived in small groups where everyone knew what you did. The group fed you as long as you did your share, so being needed feels good.Today most people work for strangers and live among strangers. Fewer people need them, so the feeling fades. They ask "what's my purpose?", and coaches and publishers sell them wrong answers. You can choose what to do with your life. You can't choose to feel needed.

The cost-benefit analysis

Your brain does a cost-benefit analysis on your effort. For nearly all of human history, work for the people around you cost effort and it paid, because the group fed you as long as you did your share. Feeling needed is the brain reporting that the effort pays. Today a wage from strangers buys the food. The effort goes to people who will never know your name, and the brain finds nothing coming back from anyone it knows. The feeling fades. A book or a course about your purpose costs an evening and asks nothing of you, so that is what sells.

The 11 details
  1. People with good jobs say nothing feels meaningful.
  2. Some people lose it when they retire.
  3. Parents of small children rarely ask the question.
  4. People lose it after moving away for a job.
  5. People who help others they know report more of it.

  9. People without it say "nobody would notice if I stopped".

  11. Only 18 percent of Americans say their job feels purposeful.

What would prove this wrong

If people with many others depending on them feel no more purpose than people with no one depending on them, this page is wrong.
