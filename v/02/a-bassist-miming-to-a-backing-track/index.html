What really is a bassist miming to a backing track?

Bassist Marcelo Perez Schneider faked his playing for decades, but nobody can be the best when they're up against every bassist on the planet.

Faking the playing is the cheapest way to the biggest prize, and exposing the fake is the cheapest way to standing and income for the exposer.

The evolutionary angle: The miming bassist

Every human wants to be the best at something. For two million years that meant the best of maybe a hundred people, and a talented guy who worked hard could get there. Online there's one top spot for everyone alive, so he faked his way into it. When an English YouTuber caught him, it stopped being about bass. Argentina defended its war veteran, Sapko brought up Maradona, and both sides picked a team like it was 1982.

Source: Viral Bassist Accused of Miming Attempts to Silence Danny Sapko: 'He Stops the Strings Before the Audio Finishes', Ultimate Guitar, 28 Oct 2026

The mismatch

Famous players praised and shared the clips, and Sapko says Billy Sheehan was among the few who saw through them. Nobody watching a clip can see the stage, and the prize on offer is the top spot among every player alive. For most of human history the people judging your skill watched you play every day, and the best of the camp was a prize years of practice could win.

Decades of practice buy the best of a small group, while a pedal and a backing track, if Sapko is right, buy the look of the best on earth for almost no work. The drive to be the best aims at whatever top spot it is shown, so that shortcut reads as a good deal. If he mimed, that is on him, and someone who says they played in his band is now accusing him.

Exposure runs on the same drive, because exposing a suspected cheat earns the catcher standing. In a camp the people who saw the deed kept the record in daily gossip, and a man who lost face could move camp and live it down. Here Sapko says a copyright strike took his first video down, and the source never says who is behind it. He posts a recap on October 27, other channels cover it, Sapko says critics get blocked, and nobody who watched both men can give a verdict.

What the brain sees: a few rivals at camp, and a top spot a hard worker can win.

What they say it is

They say

Sapko and the guitar internet call him a fraud who mimed for years, then abused copyright to bury the proof. Argentina says it's one clip and a gringo picking on a war hero. His old student calls him disturbed.

“tapping the bass at the end to con the audience”

What it really is

He isn't disturbed, because faking to keep your rank is as human as it gets.

What they get wrong. Everyone's arguing about whether he lied. He did. Nobody asks why a man who can play would fake it.

The mechanisms at work

So now what?

  1. Measure players against the people in the room: local jams, scenes and teachers where everyone sees the hands.
  2. Bookers and famous players share only playing they have seen live or in an unedited take with the hands in frame.
  3. Settle a faking charge in a room: a public live play-off judged by players who watched both men, in place of strikes and pile-ons.

On the line

See it on the line: 11,100 generations of your ancestors, then you. Gold rings: the alarm is on.

What cuts against it

  • The hunting-skill link to reproductive success is directly challenged by a later study of foraging performance, and hunting reputations track real skill only noisily. Among forager-horticulturalists, dominance as well as prestige went with higher fertility.

    • tested many timesE770 Smith 2004 E731 von Rueden & Jaeggi 2016
    • tested onceE771 Apicella 2014 E623 von Rueden et al. 2011

  • Big-game hunts rarely succeeded, so effort did not always pay off in camp, which limits how reliable being the best of the camp was as a prize.

    • tested many timesE770 Smith 2004 E731 von Rueden & Jaeggi 2016
    • contestedE810 Hawkes et al. 2014

  • Late Pleistocene social life was probably more varied than the egalitarian mobile band; some forager groups had inequality and seasonal hierarchy.

    • contestedE756 Singh & Glowacki 2022

  • Cor holds no forager record of how bands treated a faked skill display. The cheater-detection evidence is lab-based and contested, and the gossip record rests on one population.

    • tested onceE720 Wiessner 2014
    • contestedE579 Cosmides 1989 E580 Cosmides 1989

  • The source rests on unverified claims and Cor cannot tell whether he mimed. A false accusation would change what the cheater-detection signal reported on.

    • tested many timesE460 Fehr & Gächter 2002
