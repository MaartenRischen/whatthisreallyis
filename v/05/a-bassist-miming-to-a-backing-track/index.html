The miming bassist

Every human wants to be the best at something.

For two million years that meant the best of maybe a hundred people, and a talented guy who worked hard could get there. Online there's one top spot for everyone alive, so he faked his way into it. When an English YouTuber caught him, it stopped being about bass. Argentina defended its war veteran, Sapko brought up Maradona, and both sides picked a team like it was 1982.

Source: Ultimate Guitar

What they say it is

What they say

Sapko and the guitar internet call him a fraud who mimed for years, then abused copyright to bury the proof. Argentina says it's one clip and a gringo picking on a war hero. His old student calls him disturbed.

tapping the bass at the end to con the audience

What it really is

He isn't disturbed, because faking to keep your rank is as human as it gets.

What they get wrong. Everyone's arguing about whether he lied. He did. Nobody asks why a man who can play would fake it.

Famous players praised and shared the clips, and Sapko says Billy Sheehan was among the few who saw through them. Nobody watching a clip can see the stage, and the prize on offer is the top spot among every player alive. For most of human history the people judging your skill watched you play every day, and the best of the camp was a prize years of practice could win.

Decades of practice buy the best of a small group, while a pedal and a backing track, if Sapko is right, buy the look of the best on earth for almost no work. The drive to be the best aims at whatever top spot it is shown, so that shortcut reads as a good deal. If he mimed, that is on him, and someone who says they played in his band is now accusing him.

Exposure runs on the same drive, because exposing a suspected cheat earns the catcher standing. In a camp the people who saw the deed kept the record in daily gossip, and a man who lost face could move camp and live it down. Here Sapko says a copyright strike took his first video down, and the source never says who is behind it. He posts a recap on October 27, other channels cover it, Sapko says critics get blocked, and nobody who watched both men can give a verdict.

So now what?

  1. 1

    Measure players against the people in the room: local jams, scenes and teachers where everyone sees the hands.

  2. 2

    Bookers and famous players share only playing they have seen live or in an unedited take with the hands in frame.

  3. 3

    Settle a faking charge in a room: a public live play-off judged by players who watched both men, in place of strikes and pile-ons.
