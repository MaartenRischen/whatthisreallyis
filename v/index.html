Ten versions of whatreally.is

Same ten cases, same approved titles and Cor evidence in every version; only the design changes. Each picture is a phone screen: the front page, and one case page. Tap a version to open it.

All pages here are drafts and hidden from search. The live site at whatreally.is is unchanged.

  1. 05

    Tabloid front page

    A red-top tabloid front page: the red masthead block with the reversed title, the first case as the splash with its source as the main exhibit, the other nine stacked as stories with their source pictures as photo boxes.

    Case pages read like a tabloid spread: the claim line fitted huge across the full width with its "but" half in red, the source and its verbatim quotes boxed in one frame, the then and now films as a two-photo strip, numbered punch boxes with a black COR SAYS panel, and a thick-bar chart with the winner in a red reverse band.

    Based on: new design (concept assigned by the coordinator: THE TABLOID FRONT PAGE). Content is v/data only; the index hero and hero_sub come from content/headlines.json because site.json has them empty (see Known issues).

  2. 06

    Swiss grid, big data graphics

    Swiss International Typographic Style: a strict 12-column grid (4 on phone), one grotesk in three weights, black, white and one signal red, with the source screenshot and its verbatim quotes on a grey plate right under the title.

    Data is the hero: the Cor counts as giant numerals, the claims as a grid table (claim, Cor says, grade and ids), and a full-width system-wins chart with bars to scale, a real age scale and the winning score as a huge red numeral.

  3. 07

    Dark editorial magazine

    A dark photo magazine: each case opens like the first spread of a long feature, with the then film full-bleed and the claim line set large in Bodoni over it, and the source document and its verbatim quotes in one frame right under the standfirst.

    The index is a magazine cover and contents page with the source clippings as thumbnails, "What they say it is" is their white page beside our amber page, and the system-wins graph gets its own dusk-blue infographic page.

    Based on: new design (the v07 concept in the coordinator's brief: the opening spread of a long feature in a photo-led monthly). It keeps what Maarten approved or asked for: the source screenshot and verbatims in one frame high up (28 Oct build, "everything in one frame, screenshot and verbatims", OB 4005), the claim list near the top (OB 4151), Cor visible from the first second (OB 4337, the strip is sticky), "What they say it is" as a graphic of theirs beside ours (OB 4298), a plain-English and an academic line per mechanism with a link to its Cor page (31 Oct spec), films plus infographics and no per-case illustrations (OB 4296, so no pair).

  4. 09

    Scrolling story, films full-bleed

    A scrolling visual story: every case is a run of full-screen scenes, the claim line over the NOW film, then the source screenshot with its verbatim quotes, then the THEN and NOW films full-bleed with the claims stepping over them on solid cards.

    The system-wins graph stays pinned on screen while its bars grow to their scores as you scroll, and the reading sections sit on a wide two-column grid instead of a narrow strip.

    Based on: new design, built from the real system of newsroom scrollytelling (NYT "Snow Fall", The Pudding's sticky-graphic-and-steps pattern, Reuters Graphics): full-bleed media scenes, sticky media with step cards, a sticky graphic driven by scroll position, photo captions under pictures, section breaks by ground colour. Keeps the gains listed in VERSIONS-REPORT (claims with Cor says and grades, the Cor strip with live counts, approved titles verbatim, desktop width, "What they say it is", the system-wins graph) and brings back two things that were lost: the source screenshot in one frame with the verbatim quotes (28 Oct approval), and a plain-English plus academic note per Cor system with a link to its Cor page (31 Oct spec, OB via ob.md B3). No swap pairs (5 Nov rule, OB 4296).
