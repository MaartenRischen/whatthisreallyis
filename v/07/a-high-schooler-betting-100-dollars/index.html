Youth sports betting

The sick thing here is a $100 bet on one Phillies game paying the same as two days scooping ice cream. Any human body takes that deal, and the sportsbook builds it to lose. For most of human history nothing paid big for no effort, so the body still reads a quick, easy win as the real thing.

Source: Good Morning America

Rob Minnick did the sum out loud while scooping ice cream for minimum wage: $100 on one game, so why would he ever work again? Every gain once had to be walked to, dug or hunted, and a shift at the store pays a little at the end of the week. Beside that, a tap on a phone looks like the best offer he will ever get.

The sportsbook builds the deal to lose. It sets the odds and moves them during play, so lost money only comes back through another bet, and micro-bets on the next point or pitch hand out a result every few minutes with nothing that says stop. Minnick bet at least eight hours a day, went into debt six times and took a second job to keep going, and that was on him. A big hunt cost a day on foot and the camp shared the meat; the bet drops both, so every loss lands on him as private debt.

Young men chase it hardest because for most of human history a man's standing and his chances of a partner rode on risky hunts, and better hunters had more children. A minimum-wage shift offers neither. Levant is right that the product changed and brains stayed the same. Advocates want ad limits, age checks and deposit caps, and the industry says adults only, while the states took at least $3.7 billion in taxes on it in 2025. Neither camp touches the slow-paying job, which is half of the sum that makes one tap look like the best deal going.

What they say it is

They say

“Young people's brains are particularly susceptible to this because”

ABC News and its quoted clinicians

Youth betting is gambling addiction and a looming public health crisis, because young brains grow their reward parts before the parts that control behaviour.

We say

A tap that might pay two days' wages is a deal any human body takes, and the sportsbook builds it so the deal loses.

What they get wrong. The brain story explains at most why some young men tip harder, and it leaves out the slow-paying job and the product built so the next bet always looks good.

What cuts against it

  1. The constrained energy model is contested: expenditure does rise with activity at low activity levels. No Cor record states least effort as a choice rule, and forager men also hunted big game at high effort although it rarely succeeded, so the drive did not always pick the cheapest route.

    • tested once E283 Pontzer et al. 2016
    • contested E200 Lieberman 2021, E810 Hawkes et al. 2014

  2. Machine gamblers describe seeking an absorbed zone to escape anxiety, grief and boredom, so the pull may be relief more than the hunt for reward or control.

    • tested once E71 Schull 2012, E449 Schull 2012

  3. How hard and how long foragers worked is contested, and ancestral life also carried losses no action could prevent: about 49 percent of children died before 15.

    • tested many times E724 Volk & Atkinson 2013
    • tested once E761 Hawkes & O'Connell 1981
    • argued, not yet tested EEA-SUBS-02 Effort-to-outcome contingency (closed-loop, self-attributable)
    • contested EEA-SUBS-04 Daily subsistence work-hours (contested), E714 Sahlins 1972

  4. Substance-specific addiction research is a real challenge: a small trial found low-dose semaglutide cut alcohol intake in the lab but not drinking days, so the cue-and-loop reading does not cover every pattern of compulsive use.

    • tested once E873 Hendershot et al. 2025

  5. Cor holds no record of gambling or betting among mobile foragers, so the claim that bets hijack the energy and seeking systems rests on the architecture and on machine-gambling ethnography alone.

    • tested many times E73 Schull 2012
    • tested once E71 Schull 2012
    • ungraded C6 Wanting-Liking Dissociation as Proxy Hijack

  6. Constructed-emotion work finds no consistent brain or body fingerprint for any single emotion, which limits the claim that seeking is one conserved circuit.

    • tested many times E300 Barrett et al. 2019, E394 Panksepp & Biven 2012
    • argued, not yet tested E301 Barrett 2017, E304 Barrett 2017

Not in Cor yet

  • Chasing losses, betting again to win back what was lost, is a core feature of gambling problems.

    Lesieur 1979, The compulsive gambler's spiral of options and involvement

  • In-play betting is linked to higher rates of problem gambling than betting on final outcomes.

    Killick & Griffiths 2019, In-play sports betting: a scoping study

  • In forager bands, a hunter who came home empty still ate from others' kills, so failed hunts were absorbed by the group.

    Kaplan, Hill and colleagues on food sharing as risk reduction among foragers (e.g. Kaplan & Hill 1985; Cashdan 1985, Coping with risk)
