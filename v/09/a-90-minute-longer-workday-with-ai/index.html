AI users work longer

Source: ActivTrak Productivity Lab: AI Users Have Workdays Up to 90 Minutes Longer Than Non-Users, prnewswire.com, 29 Oct 2026

AI may help you finish faster, and you still work later, because a job never says that's enough for today.

The company selling your boss the software that logs it all calls that a tool problem. Your body still runs the program that kept us alive and fed our children, the most gain for the least energy, so you grab the tool and the job keeps the time it saved.

What they say it is

They say

AI contributes to longer workdays, more app switching and shorter focus, standalone browser tools add a swivel-chair tax of extra switching, so leaders should embed AI in existing apps and treat its use as a means to measurable business gains.

potentially adding friction as they move between tools to complete their work.

ActivTrak and the head of its Productivity Lab

What it really is

Workers take the cheapest way to get each task done, and a job has no point where today is covered, so finishing faster never ends the day.

What they get wrong. Tool design accounts for part of the switching and says nothing about why AI users' days run longer, which comes from a job with no point where today's work is done.

The rest

AI users log 8.4 to 9.1 hours a day against 7.6 for non-users, while heavy users' share of the day after hours barely moves, 32.3 percent against 31.9 for non-users. The headline's 90 minutes is the top of that range, and the vendor's own executive says AI may help people finish tasks faster, yet the release still finds longer days. The body takes the cheapest route to any task, and all the release shows is what comes after: a longer day. For most of human history effort was self-paced and extra food went to children and campmates; a job has no point where today is covered.

The release counts anything outside the scheduled start and end times as after hours, so a clock decides when the day ends. Camp work rose and fell with what the day needed, while a job pays for a clocked schedule.

ActivTrak sells employers the software that logs all this and ran the study on its own data. Its fix is to measure AI use against business gains, which is its product. Calling the switching a swivel-chair tax makes a job with no end look like a tool fault you fix by buying more measuring.

So now what?

  1. Agree a set amount of output per day with your team and manager, and log off once it is delivered, so finishing faster ends the day early.
  2. Get the team, a works council or a union to put the scheduled hours, the after-hours window and the monitoring settings on the table, so the people doing the work help set them.
  3. Move toward a contract or an employer that pays for finished work and lets you walk off once it is delivered, so the saved time stays yours.

What cuts against it

  1. The constrained energy model is contested: total spending does rise with activity at low activity levels, the idea that the body pays for activity by cutting other spending is still a hypothesis, and two weeks of forced sitting added organ fat in healthy young men.

    • tested once E283 Pontzer et al. 2016 E202 Lieberman 2021
    • contested E200 Lieberman 2021

  2. How many hours foragers worked has no agreed number: one reading finds a few hours of food-getting, another far more once processing is counted, so the modern day of 7.6 to 9.1 hours cannot be compared with it as a number.

    • tested once E761 Hawkes & O'Connell 1981
    • contested EEA-SUBS-04 Daily subsistence work-hours (contested) E714 Sahlins 1972

  3. Cor holds no record for the most gain for the least energy as a choice rule; the energy records show intake bounds energy and do not show the body picks the cheapest route to each task. The choice rule is staged for tier 2.

    • tested many times E284 Pontzer et al. 2016 EEA-DIET-04 Energy regulation is intake-bounded (total expenditure is homeostatically buffered, not behavior-throttled)
    • tested once E283 Pontzer et al. 2016 E286 Pontzer et al. 2012

  4. Ancestral life carried losses no action could prevent: about 49 percent of children died before 15, and sickness, with infections prominent, caused most deaths, so low control is not new.

    • tested many times E724 Volk & Atkinson 2013 E725 Gurven & Kaplan 2007

  5. Effort that goes unrewarded predicts poorer health independently of low control, and felt control and managers' ratings of control barely correlate; the control reading rests on observational data and rodent circuit work.

    • tested many times E166 Marmot 2004
    • tested once E161 Marmot 2004
    • seen in animals E441 Maier & Seligman 2016

  6. Cor holds no forager evidence on task-switching rate or focus-session length, so the contrast on interruption is a tier-2 gap.

    • argued, not yet tested EEA-SUBS-03 Task diversity within the subsistence day

  7. How much of a hunter's kill went to his own household is disputed, and much sharing happened under demand; the surplus-transfer claim itself stands.

    • tested once E708 Kaplan & Hill 1985
    • argued, not yet tested E709 Peterson 1993
    • contested E781 Hawkes et al. 2001
