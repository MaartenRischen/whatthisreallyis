What really is a 90 minute longer workday with AI?

whatreally.is/a-90-minute-longer-workday-with-ai

AI does work twice as fast, but the modern world never runs out of work.

Workers take the cheapest route to getting work done, and the job pockets the saved energy as extra hours instead of letting them stop.

AI users work longer

AI may help you finish faster, and you still work later, because a job never says that's enough for today. The company selling your boss the software that logs it all calls that a tool problem. Your body still runs the program that kept us alive and fed our children, the most gain for the least energy, so you grab the tool and the job keeps the time it saved.

Source: ActivTrak Productivity Lab: AI Users Have Workdays Up to 90 Minutes Longer Than Non-Users, prnewswire.com, 29 Oct 2026

What they say it is

They say

AI contributes to longer workdays, more app switching and shorter focus, standalone browser tools add a swivel-chair tax of extra switching, so leaders should embed AI in existing apps and treat its use as a means to measurable business gains.

“potentially adding friction as they move between tools to complete their work.”

ActivTrak and the head of its Productivity Lab

What it really is

Workers take the cheapest way to get each task done, and a job has no point where today is covered, so finishing faster never ends the day.

What they get wrong. Tool design accounts for part of the switching and says nothing about why AI users' days run longer, which comes from a job with no point where today's work is done.

The rest

AI users log 8.4 to 9.1 hours a day against 7.6 for non-users, while heavy users' share of the day after hours barely moves, 32.3 percent against 31.9 for non-users. The headline's 90 minutes is the top of that range, and the vendor's own executive says AI may help people finish tasks faster, yet the release still finds longer days. The body takes the cheapest route to any task, and all the release shows is what comes after: a longer day. For most of human history effort was self-paced and extra food went to children and campmates; a job has no point where today is covered.

The release counts anything outside the scheduled start and end times as after hours, so a clock decides when the day ends. Camp work rose and fell with what the day needed, while a job pays for a clocked schedule.

ActivTrak sells employers the software that logs all this and ran the study on its own data. Its fix is to measure AI use against business gains, which is its product. Calling the switching a swivel-chair tax makes a job with no end look like a tool fault you fix by buying more measuring.

So now what?

  1. Agree a set amount of output per day with your team and manager, and log off once it is delivered, so finishing faster ends the day early.

  2. Get the team, a works council or a union to put the scheduled hours, the after-hours window and the monitoring settings on the table, so the people doing the work help set them.

  3. Move toward a contract or an employer that pays for finished work and lets you walk off once it is delivered, so the saved time stays yours.
