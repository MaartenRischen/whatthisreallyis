Cor is the core of this project: an evidence atlas of what humans need, drawn from thousands of studies in evolutionary biology, anthropology, primatology, neuroscience, psychology and psychiatry. Every decode, every page and every Decode Talk conversation is made from it. AI compiles the studies into Cor and uses Cor to do the decoding, more objectively than any human can.

The three checks, for every finding in Cor:

  1. Every finding is checked against the study's full text or abstract. Summaries, search snippets and Wikipedia don't count.
  2. The finding says no more than the study does.
  3. Every grade names the strongest study behind it and that study's main weakness.

A grade never goes up because it would help a page. A finding that failed to replicate is marked down, however widely it is cited. Cor keeps records of the evidence against itself, publishes the strongest attacks on its own work and names the results that would kill it.

Humans have existed for 300,000 years. For nearly all of that time a man lived in a camp of about 30 people, inside a pool of about a thousand people he would meet in his whole life. Men earned standing by skill that others chose to respect, and by fear, and the camp put down any man who tried to rule the rest. Then people started farming, and villages grew into towns and cities. That began 12,000 years ago. Today a man is ranked among millions, mostly by strangers, through titles, pay and audience size. A city built so that one man can climb from the bottom to the top in an afternoon is new.

In five forager societies, better hunters had more children than other men in their group. Among the Hadza, stronger men had a better name as hunters and more children.

The cost-benefit analysis

Your brain does a cost-benefit analysis on every route to the top. The real climb costs years, it can fail in front of people who know you, and the group stops a man who tries to take the top by force. What it brought in, for nearly all of human history, was standing with those people, and with it a mate and more children. The climb in GTA costs the price of the game and an afternoon. It can't fail, and nobody stops you. It brings in the signs of the top: the cars, the money, a city that reacts to you. The signs come cheap, so the brain takes the game. What changed is the cost of the real route. The few dozen people who knew you are gone, standing is ranked among millions, and a company built a climb that costs almost nothing. The hours it takes come out of the time with people where the real climb happens.

So now what

  1. Standing that counts is standing with people who know you. Find a group small enough that people see what you are good at: a team, a crew, a club, a workshop. Go every week.
  2. Get good at one thing that other people come to you for, and do it for them.
  3. Play with people you know. A crew of friends, the same people every week, is real time with real people. The rank, the cars and the money in the game are the stand-in.
  4. Count what the game took this week: sleep, people, work, a date.
  5. Don't buy game money. It sells you the climb for real money, and nothing it buys leaves the game.
  6. If the game is where you feel most like a man worth something, take that as the signal. What is missing is standing among people who know you.

What cuts against it

  • Sex on a man's mind does not make him take risks or show off.
