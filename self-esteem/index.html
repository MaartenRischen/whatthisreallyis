What it really is

It doesn't exist. Esteem always comes from other people, or from an audience you imagine.

What people call self-esteem is your reading of what the people around you think of you. Your body keeps that reading all day, because for nearly all of human history your standing with a few dozen people decided who shared food with you, who backed you and who would have you as a mate.It rises and falls with how those people treat you, and being left out hits it hardest. You can't make it yourself. People who rated themselves low and repeated "I'm a lovable person" felt worse. Since the 1980s schools and self-help have sold it as something you build inside, alone.Now most people live among strangers, so the reading comes from people who don't know you, or from an audience you imagine. Nothing in you is broken. A low reading is a correct reading of a life with too few people who know and value you.

The part of you that tracks where you stand is 420 million years old.Learn moreClose
Fish have it. Humans have existed for 300,000 years, and for nearly all of that time you were judged by a few dozen people who knew you and saw what you did. Then people started farming and settling down, and villages grew into towns and cities. That began 12,000 years ago. Now you are judged by strangers, by numbers on a screen and by an audience you imagine. The reading drops, and a health service tells you to see yourself differently.

What it is not

An opinion you hold of yourselfHealth services: "self-esteem is the opinion we have of ourselves."It moves with what other people do. In five studies, how people felt about themselves went up and down with whether they expected others to accept or reject them. In 120 experiments, being left out of a ball game on a screen by players they had never met was enough to knock it down. An opinion of your own would not need anyone else.

How it happens

  1. 1

    Your standing decided who fed you, backed you and would have you.

    The science says

    Your standing with the people around you decided who shared food with you, who backed you and who would have you as a mate. So your body tracks it all day.

    Across 33 societies without industry, men with higher standing had more children. In a Tsimane village in Bolivia, the number of allies a man had explained most of his influence. Leary named the tracker the sociometer: it watches for signs that people value you less, and warns you.

The cost-benefit analysis

Your brain does a cost-benefit analysis on showing yourself. Being seen by the people around you can raise what they think of you, and it can lower it. Hiding protects the reading you have. For nearly all of human history hiding wasn't possible. A few dozen people saw what you did every day, and you could change what they thought by what you did next. Now you are judged by people who don't know you and by numbers on a screen. Working on yourself alone, with a book or a course, costs little and risks nothing. The brain picks that, and the reading doesn't move, because people who can't see you can't value you.

Real example cases

If I stop performing, will I still be enough?

Dion Elliott Jensen in Men's Health Australia, after losing his job

He writes that his title had become his worth, and that without the role he no longer knew how to introduce himself. A title is other people's rating of you. When the job went, the people who gave the rating went with it.

relying on others' approval and behavior toward me

A writer for NAMI, the US mental health alliance, on what lockdown took away

The writer calls that a problem and concludes that the love has to come from oneself. It is how every human measures it. Lockdown took the people away, and the reading fell.

It was quasi-religious

Will Storr in The Guardian, 2017, on the California task force of 1986 (linked through Longreads)

A California politician got a state task force funded to promote it as the answer to crime, drug use and school failure. According to Storr, the task force's publicists misrepresented what its professors had found.

So now what

  1. Stop working on what you think of yourself. Work on who knows you.
  2. Be of use to people who will see it: cook, fix, coach, carry. Standing comes from being useful to people who know you.
  3. Spend your time with a few people who see you every week. Theirs is the opinion your body is built to read.
  4. A count of likes is a reading from strangers. Give it the weight you would give a stranger's opinion.
  5. When the reading is low, ask whose opinion it is: someone who knows you, a stranger, or nobody at all.

Don't repeat to yourself that you are worth it. In experiments that made people who rated themselves low feel worse. If you are thinking about ending your life, tell someone today and call your local emergency number.

Which system wins

Every animal on a branch past a number has the part named for it below. A system has several parts, and they differ in age.

  1. 1

    Being wanted as a mate

    Sexual hormone axis, 460 million years. Shared with every animal that has a backbone.

    Cor M14, Reproductive Motivation: 523 graded findings
  2. 2

    Tracking where you stand with the people around you Wins

    Rank circuit, 420 million years. Shared with fish, reptiles, birds and mammals.

    Cor M5, Status Monitoring: 549 graded findings
  3. 3

    Being accepted by your own people

    Separation distress, 165 million years. Shared with mammals.

    Cor M3, Social Bonding: 1161 graded findings

Not drawn to scale. The ages and the animals are Cor's.

Why it wins. Tracking where you stand explains 5 of the 12 details: the drop when a job goes, the rise with approval, the titles and performances people measure by, the hiding and the low mood. Being accepted by your own people explains 4: the drop when a partner leaves, the approval, the fall in lockdown and the part childhood plays. The search for a mate explains one detail, so it doesn't lead. Four details are explained by none of them: the idea that it is your own opinion, the advice to build it alone, the task force and the courses. Those are the product.

What would prove this wrong

If people can raise how they feel about themselves for good, with no change in how anyone around them treats them, this page is wrong.

A study can settle this.

Strongest objection"So I'm supposed to care what everyone thinks of me?" No. You are built to care what a few dozen people who know you think. A stranger's opinion was never part of the reading, and a follower count is a count of strangers.
